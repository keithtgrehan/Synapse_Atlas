## Weekly pass rule

A week is complete only when all are true:

- Quiz score is **80% or higher**.
- You can explain the core topic aloud for five minutes without notes.
- One paper critique is complete.
- One trial teardown or programme analysis is complete.
- One product contribution is reviewed and source-linked.
- Every material factual claim has a source.
- The `ERROR_LOG.md` is updated.

### Remediation

- **80–100%:** advance.
- **70–79%:** correct every missed item and retest within 48 hours.
- **Below 70%:** stop new content for one study session, rebuild the topic, then retest.
- A high average cannot compensate for failing a hard checkpoint.

## Evidence labels

Every note, product record, and publication claim must be labelled as one of:

```text
SOURCE_FACT
STRUCTURED_EXTRACTION
DERIVED_CALCULATION
HUMAN_INFERENCE
MODEL_INFERENCE
UNVERIFIED
UNKNOWN
```

Rules:

- A model inference never becomes a source fact automatically.
- A company statement remains a **company claim** unless independently supported.
- Absence of reported evidence is not evidence of absence.
- Mechanistic plausibility is not clinical efficacy.
- Statistical significance is not necessarily clinical significance.
- “Open access” does not mean all reuse conditions are identical.
- Conflicts and uncertainty are preserved rather than silently reconciled.

# 5. Day-0 baseline test

Complete closed-book before starting Week 1. Score one point for each materially correct answer.

1. Distinguish affinity, potency, efficacy, and selectivity.
2. Distinguish pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics.
3. What is primarily learned in Phase I, Phase II, and Phase III?
4. What is an IND in the US and a CTA in the EU?
5. Why can functional unblinding inflate an apparent treatment effect?
6. What does a confidence interval communicate that a p-value does not?
7. Explain intention-to-treat analysis.
8. What is multiplicity?
9. Primary endpoint versus exploratory endpoint?
10. What is receptor occupancy?
11. Why can a clinical effect outlast plasma exposure?
12. What is biased agonism or functional selectivity?
13. What does a composition-of-matter patent protect?
14. Distinguish licensing, co-development, acquisition, and research collaboration.
15. Why can a promising Phase II study fail in Phase III?
16. What is CMC and why can it block a programme?
17. Distinguish source data, extracted claim, calculation, and inference.
18. Why is a registry record usually stronger evidence of registered study status than a company pipeline page?
19. What is a clinically meaningful effect?
20. Name three reasons psychedelic trials are difficult to blind.

## Baseline interpretation

| Score | Interpretation |
|---:|---|
| 0–7 | Rebuild fundamentals aggressively |
| 8–13 | Useful scientific base; material industry gaps |
| 14–17 | Strong base; focus on evidence and development detail |
| 18–20 | Accelerate, but do not skip applied outputs |

## Reference answer points

1. Affinity = binding strength; potency = concentration/dose required; efficacy = maximum functional effect; selectivity = preference relative to other targets.
2. PK = what the body does to the drug; PD = what the drug does to the body.
3. Phase I: safety/tolerability and PK/PD; Phase II: dose and preliminary efficacy; Phase III: confirmatory benefit-risk.
4. IND is the US investigational-new-drug mechanism; CTA is an EU clinical-trial application.
5. Allocation knowledge changes expectancy, reporting, care, ratings, and co-interventions.
6. A confidence interval communicates compatible effect sizes and precision.
7. Analyse participants according to randomised assignment to preserve randomisation as far as possible.
8. Testing multiple hypotheses increases false-positive risk unless controlled.
9. Primary endpoint tests the main prespecified hypothesis; exploratory endpoints generate further hypotheses.
10. Proportion of available receptors bound at a specified exposure/time.
11. Active metabolites, tissue/brain kinetics, receptor residence, downstream signalling, plasticity, or learning can outlast plasma exposure.
12. The same receptor can preferentially activate different downstream pathways depending on the ligand/context.
13. The claimed chemical entity, subject to validity and claim scope.
14. Licensing grants defined rights; co-development shares work/economics; acquisition transfers ownership/control; collaboration performs specified joint work.
15. Small/selected samples, weak controls, inflated effects, operational changes, heterogeneity, multiplicity, and chance.
16. Chemistry, manufacturing, and controls; formulation, impurity, stability, quality, or scale-up failure can stop development.
17. Source data are original records; extraction transforms them; calculation applies a formula; inference interprets.
18. Registries use structured formal status fields; company pages are sponsor-controlled and may be promotional or stale.
19. An effect large enough to matter in the relevant patient and benefit-risk context.
20. Noticeable subjective effects, expectancy, prior experience, therapist/rater inference, and weak active controls.

Retake this test on Day 90.

# 9. Career track running in parallel

## Weeks 1–2

- Build the first product vertical slice.
- Create a list of 100 target organisations:
  - psychedelic biotech;
  - broader CNS biotech;
  - CROs;
  - pharma neuroscience;
  - AI drug discovery;
  - research organisations;
  - specialist investors.
- Begin two domain conversations per week.

## Weeks 3–6

- Five conversations per week.
- Share the private demo selectively.
- Ask about actual workflow and partnership problems.
- Record repeated problems; build only repeated high-impact needs.
- Produce the first publication.

## Weeks 7–13

- Direct founder/CBO/partnership outreach.
- Twenty high-quality applications or direct approaches.
- Referral asks based on demonstrated work.
- One target-specific briefing per serious application.
- Maintain a smaller conventional GTM pipeline as downside protection.

The product is a career asset, not a startup fundraising exercise.

# 10. Time sinks to reject

Do not spend the 90 days on:

- another degree;
- advanced synthetic chemistry;
- full medical-school neuroanatomy;
- speculative consciousness theory;
- generic “AI in healthcare” certificates;
- a broad RAG chatbot;
- molecule generation;
- patent-law opinions;
- full-text warehousing;
- custom model training;
- decorative dashboards;
- social-media hype;
- unsourced psychedelic advocacy.

The objective is **scientifically credible commercial and product fluency with visible proof**.
